Dividend-Paying Tokens on Robinhood Chain

INDEX, BUCKET and RBTX: how each one turns trading activity into stock-token payouts, what you have to hold, and what to verify. Data as of Oct 31, 2026.

What a "dividend token" actually is

None of these tokens receive a dividend from a company. Each one takes a fee stream (from trading, or from a launchpad), uses it to buy Robinhood Stock Tokens (NVDA, SPCX, AAPL and so on), and sends them to wallets that qualify. The payout is therefore only as steady as three things: the fee stream, the rules for who qualifies, and whoever runs the payout machinery.

Stock Tokens are tokenized debt securities issued by Robinhood Assets (Jersey) Limited. They track the stock price but do not give legal ownership of the shares.

The payout chain, and where it breaks

1 · ActivityTrading volume or launchpad fees. Falls when attention falls.
2 · Fee captureToken tax, pool hook, or a fee wallet. Who can change it?
3 · Stock purchaseA keeper or "machine" buys the stocks. Slippage and timing matter.
4 · AllocationThresholds, snapshots, minimum payout sizes. Decides if you get paid.
5 · DeliveryTransfer to your wallet. Is it on-chain and checkable?

Three different plumbing models. INDEX taxes the token itself. BUCKET uses a fee hook inside its own pool plus an engine. RBTX has no token tax and relies on a fee wallet. A token tax and a pool hook are written into contracts. A fee wallet that "sends out" payouts is a team decision unless the routing is enforced on chain, and I could not verify that.
Shared risks. Payouts fall when volume falls. Thresholds are set in dollars or tokens that move with price. Small holders can qualify and still get cents. All three are small-cap, thin-liquidity tokens that can lose most of their value quickly.

INDEX Token-tax dividend

How it pays

A 3% ETH tax on every INDEX trade buys a basket of tokenized stocks and sends them pro-rata to eligible wallets every 15 minutes, with no staking or claiming. Sources list the basket at anywhere from 5 to 18 stocks; the latest post you shared says 18.

Ecosystem beyond the token

  • Blend: baskets of stocks and memes. The Oct 8 relaunch says dividends stay inside the basket, one trade buys every underlying asset, and owner fees can go back into holder backing.
  • Indices (lets other tokens build treasuries that turn fees into stock rewards, buybacks or liquidity) and Earn-A-Stock (launch product).
  • Listings: reported added to Robinhood's tradable assets on Sep 11; trading on Moonshot.
  • In my X search the Index account's last original post was Oct 8 (plus one repost on Oct 28). Search may be incomplete.
Bull case
  • It has the longest payout record of the three: $1.66M in stocks distributed.
  • 98% of supply is already circulating, so no scheduled unlock is hanging over holders.
  • Fee-to-stock works automatically when volume returns, and a restart with $6K waiting shows the machine is running.
  • Blend and Indices give it revenue routes beyond one token.
Counter-case and open questions
  • The price fell more than 90% from September. The cause is unproven: dilution, insider selling, manipulation and a rug are hypotheses, not findings.
  • "Fees never stopped" conflicts with DefiLlama's $0 over 24 hours. A pause and restart would explain both, but check.
  • Competitors (Bucket, Robotics Reserve) pitch against it, and Robotics Reserve's community posts compare itself directly with INDEX.
  • One reply mentions "URC-5" possibly changing INDEX. I found no confirmation.

If you want to investigate the crash without accusing anyone

  1. Get the holder list just before the first big leg down.
  2. Rank wallets by net selling in the crash window.
  3. Exclude pools, routers and the tax and distribution contracts.
  4. Trace each top seller's funding and token source.
  5. Label an address an insider only with a demonstrable link (shared funder, deployer, labelled treasury, multisig).
  6. Compare against liquidity depth: ordinary holders can crash a shallow pool.
  7. Publish the method and addresses.

BUCKET Hook-fee engine

How it pays

A 3% fee on BUCKET/ETH trades (1.5% in the USDG pool) flows to a Treasury. An engine buys stocks, and a Drop lands in your wallet when your accrual crosses $2. You choose a Bucket (a basket) and cards can multiply or redirect your payout. Eligibility is checked on chain at send time; amounts and asset choices are computed off chain by the keeper.

What is new since the last guide

  • Fantasy Draft (Oct 30): the official post says the first draft wrapped. Leagues are customizable "on the same level as ESPN fantasy football" with a bench, add/drop, trades and swaps, and at season end players "settle up however you want with your friends." The post says nothing about fees or Drops.
  • Drip BUCKET (Oct 29): deposit BUCKET and hold dripBUCKET. A liquidity band sells BUCKET into ETH as price rises and re-bids below. Fees compound into the share, 20% of trading fees go to Drops, and withdrawals return BUCKET plus ETH. Bucket notes a share is worth 0.993 BUCKET and about 4% more in dollars at that moment, so ETH moves matter too.
  • 8 new Stock Tokens: JPM, V, PYPL, BMNR, MCD, DIS, UBER, GDX can be put in custom Buckets, elected on cards and swapped.
  • Pick'em: weekly winners announced Oct 30, including a team named "Claude's decision" in 3rd.

Controls, from Bucket's own rules page (dated Sep 15)

  • Token contract: no owner, mint or pause. 1B supply, with about 10.9M BUCKET burned. The 3% fee is a constant. Liquidity is locked with no removal path.
  • One owner key holds every owner role and signs the keeper loop every 5 minutes. No multisig or timelock.
  • The owner can withdraw Treasury ETH and sweep the FeeVault; 20M BUCKET was swept in September to fund operations.
  • Payout amounts, asset choice and the $2 threshold are decided off chain by the keeper.
  • Audit by SB Security: 31 findings, 28 fixed, all critical and high closed (site).
Bull case
  • The most documented control surface of the three, with a published list of who can change what.
  • Many revenue lines: swap fees, Drip, launchpad, cards, Pick'em, now the Fantasy Draft.
  • It sells its engine to other tokens, and the choice of stock per holder is a real differentiator.
  • The site's reported payouts are on-chain transfers anyone can check against the ledger.
Counter-case and open questions
  • Fantasy Draft fees are unconfirmed. The tweet that sparked this says so itself, and the official post is silent. A game people "settle up" privately may produce no protocol fee at all.
  • "Every trade pays BUCKET holders" overstates it: the site says hops touching a stock token are fee-free with no rebate. Holders are paid by BUCKET trades.
  • Threshold conflict: 10,000 BUCKET on chain versus 65,000 in the app. Confirm before you size anything.
  • Single-key trust and the 20M BUCKET sweep are the central risks.
  • Fetched pages may be cached: the payout counter read the same $154,492 on Oct 26 and Oct 31.

RBTX · Robotics Reserve Fee-wallet payouts

Identified as the Robotics Reserve token (@RoboticsReserve, contract 0xd5e8…0723 as posted by the team). If you meant a different RBTX, tell me. The project's own website was blocked for me, so this section rests on its X posts, market pages and outside analysts.

How it pays

  • Daily "URI" payouts go to wallets holding $100 or more of RBTX at the snapshot, or an activated Robot Mage NFT. No staking.
  • A daily 24-hour community vote picks the stock for that round (recent: SPCX, NVDA).
  • The team says the "Pons fee wallet sends out URI stock distributions daily" and that there are no team tokens to lock.
  • Latest posts: 4.247 SPCX to 133 eligible wallets; an NVDA round to 127 wallets; $15,150 distributed across 23 payouts; "over $14k in the last few weeks."

Outside scrutiny (@wisehw25, Oct 30; an analyst who also promotes a newsletter)

  • The "$100 minimum" is dollar-denominated, so a $105 balance can drop below $100 at the snapshot without selling a token.
  • The owner chooses snapshot timing, allocations depend on actual revenue, and rewards can be zero.
  • An earlier site view showed about $3,115 in reserve value and $12,936 in distributions, and another panel said "No recorded drops." That is a discrepancy to reconcile, not proof of anything. The newer $15,150 figure should be checked the same way.
  • The reserve is informational and not redeemable, and not a price floor, per the site's own wording as quoted.

Arithmetic on the reported numbers

  • $15,150 ÷ 23 payouts ≈ $659 per payout. Split across about 130 wallets, that is roughly $5 per wallet on average.
  • $659 a day for a year is about $240K, close to the whole $243K market cap. So either launch-level volume continues, or payouts shrink. Do not annualize it.
  • The claim "most stock per holding size on Robinhood Chain" can only be checked wallet by wallet against the other tokens. A small eligible group sharing the same pot makes per-holder payouts look large.

The Office and covered calls (announced Oct 29–30, "early next week")

  • Fully collateralized covered calls: a seller escrows tokens and sets premium, exercise price and expiry. The buyer pays the premium, and can exercise for the exercise amount in ETH.
  • Premiums and exercise payments are in ETH; the platform takes 10% of the premium; manual exercise only; no resale of positions; settlement delivers tokens, not company shares.
  • Launch lineup: RBTX, INDEX, BUCKET, STONKBROKER, NET, PONS, NVDA, SPCX. That puts all three tokens in this guide on one options venue.
  • The team's own Oct 29 post says an independent security audit, legal assessment and real token-compatibility checks are still required before launch.
  • A covered call caps your upside above the strike while you keep the downside. Buyers can lose the whole premium.
Bull case
  • Active shipping: the Office, covered calls, daily payouts with named rounds and wallet counts.
  • The token itself is simple: renounced, not mintable, 0% tax, so there is no token-level tax trap.
  • Low entry point ($100) and an NFT route to eligibility, and a vote that gives holders a say in the stock.
  • Options give the three tokens another use beyond holding.
Counter-case and open questions
  • It is the smallest and youngest: about $243K cap, 335 holders, about $47K of liquidity.
  • Payouts depend on a team-run fee wallet and a team-chosen snapshot rather than a contract rule I could verify.
  • Payout totals are self-reported and I have not reconciled them to transactions.
  • Options are not live and not audited. Fresh options markets on thin tokens can be manipulated.
  • Several promotional accounts post payout screenshots. Treat those as marketing.

A verification checklist for any dividend token

  1. Find three real payout transactions and check recipient, asset and amount against the project's post.
  2. Count the wallets that actually received tokens and compare with the claimed number.
  3. Read the exact snapshot rule: when it is taken, who requests it, what happens if you cross the threshold just before or after.
  4. Check whether the threshold is in tokens or dollars, and what happens to it if the price halves.
  5. Find the minimum payout per asset (INDEX skips about $0.03 per stock; BUCKET pays at $2).
  6. Ask what happens to the fee stream if the owner key is lost or the team stops.
  7. Check that the received stock token has a pool you can sell into.
  8. Compare against the payouts a holder of your size would see on a quiet day, not a launch day.
